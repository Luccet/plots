:::meta title: Areas between curves lang: en ::: # Areas between curves > An example of the [plots kit's](https://github.com/Luccet/plots) `plot.js`: functions of $x$ > and of $y$, a definition or two, regions between two graphs and inside implicit curves, > polar and parametric curves, an implicit circle, labelled points, legends with formulas, > ticks in $\pi$, and plots as numbered figures. The colours are the > [classic theme's](https://github.com/Luccet/themes), picked by number. The note loads > `../plot.js`, this repository's copy. ## Between two graphs Where $f \ge g$ on $[a, b]$, the area between their graphs is $$ A = \int_a^b \bigl(f(x) - g(x)\bigr)\,dx. $$ {#eq-between} $\sin x$ and $\cos x$ cross where $\tan x = 1$, at $x = \pi/4$ and $x = 5\pi/4$, and between the two crossings $\sin x \ge \cos x$ (@fig-sincos). So $$ \begin{aligned} A &= \int_{\pi/4}^{5\pi/4} (\sin x - \cos x)\,dx = \Bigl[-\cos x - \sin x\Bigr]_{\pi/4}^{5\pi/4} \\ &= \sqrt2 + \sqrt2 = 2\sqrt2 \approx 2.83. \end{aligned} $$ :::figure[The region between $\sin x$ and $\cos x$, from one crossing to the next.]{#fig-sincos} ```plot f(x) = sin x g(x) = cos x g(x) <= y <= f(x), x in [π/4, 5π/4] {color=3 label="$A = 2\sqrt{2}$"} y = f(x) {color=1 label="$\sin x$"} y = g(x) {color=4 label="$\cos x$"} (π/4, sqrt(2)/2) {color=5 label="π/4"} (5π/4, -sqrt(2)/2) {color=5 label="5π/4"} x in [-π/4, 9π/4] y in [-1.5, 1.5] ``` ::: ## Slicing along y The parabola $x = y^2$ and the line $x = y + 2$ meet where $y^2 = y + 2$, at $y = -1$ and $y = 2$: the points $(1, -1)$ and $(4, 2)$ of @fig-slices. Sliced across $x$, the region needs two integrals, since its lower edge changes at $x = 1$ (dashed): the parabola's lower half before it, the line after. Sliced along $y$ it needs one, the line being to the right of the parabola all the way from $y = -1$ to $y = 2$. By @eq-between with $x$ and $y$ swapped, $$ A = \int_{-1}^{2} (y + 2 - y^2)\,dy = \Bigl[\tfrac{y^2}{2} + 2y - \tfrac{y^3}{3}\Bigr]_{-1}^{2} = \tfrac{10}{3} + \tfrac{7}{6} = \tfrac{9}{2}. $$ The two integrals in $x$ agree: $$ \int_0^1 2\sqrt{x}\,dx + \int_1^4 (\sqrt{x} - x + 2)\,dx = \tfrac43 + \tfrac{19}{6} = \tfrac92. $$ :::figure[The region between $x = y^2$ and $x = y + 2$.]{#fig-slices} ```plot y^2 <= x <= y + 2 {color=3 label="$A = 9/2$"} x = y^2 {color=1 label="$x = y^2$"} x = y + 2 {color=4 label="$x = y + 2$"} x = 1, y in [-1, 1] {color=5 .dashed} (1, -1), (4, 2) {color=5} x in [-1, 5] y in [-2.5, 3] ``` ::: ## In polar coordinates The area swept by $r = r(\theta)$ as $\theta$ runs from $\alpha$ to $\beta$ is $\frac12 \int_\alpha^\beta r^2\,d\theta$. The circle $r = 3\cos\theta$ and the cardioid $r = 1 + \cos\theta$ meet where $\cos\theta = \frac12$, at $\theta = \pm\pi/3$ and $r = \frac32$: the points $\bigl(\frac34, \pm\frac{3\sqrt3}{4}\bigr)$. Inside the circle and outside the cardioid, $$ \begin{aligned} A &= \frac12 \int_{-\pi/3}^{\pi/3} \bigl(9\cos^2\theta - (1 + \cos\theta)^2\bigr)\,d\theta \\ &= \frac12 \int_{-\pi/3}^{\pi/3} (3 + 4\cos 2\theta - 2\cos\theta)\,d\theta \\ &= \frac12 \bigl(2\pi + 2\sqrt3 - 2\sqrt3\bigr) = \pi. \end{aligned} $$ @fig-polar shades the points that are in both: inside the circle, $x^2 + y^2 < 3x$, and outside the cardioid, $(x^2 + y^2 - x)^2 > x^2 + y^2$. A point is in both when the smaller of $3x - x^2 - y^2$ and $(x^2 + y^2 - x)^2 - x^2 - y^2$ is positive, which is one inequality. :::figure[Inside the circle $r = 3\cos\theta$, outside the cardioid $r = 1 + \cos\theta$.]{#fig-polar} ```plot min(3x - x^2 - y^2, (x^2 + y^2 - x)^2 - x^2 - y^2) > 0 {color=3 label="$A = \pi$"} r = 3 cos θ {color=1 label="$r = 3\cos\theta$"} r = 1 + cos θ {color=4 label="$r = 1 + \cos\theta$"} (3/4, 3sqrt(3)/4), (3/4, -3sqrt(3)/4) {color=5} equal x in [-1, 4] y in [-1.75, 1.75] ``` ::: ## A closed parametric curve A closed curve traced once anticlockwise encloses $A = \frac12 \oint (x\,dy - y\,dx)$. For the astroid $x = \cos^3 t$, $y = \sin^3 t$, $$ x\,dy - y\,dx = 3\sin^2 t \cos^2 t\,(\cos^2 t + \sin^2 t)\,dt = \tfrac34 \sin^2 2t\,dt, $$ so $$ A = \frac12 \int_0^{2\pi} \tfrac34 \sin^2 2t\,dt = \frac{3\pi}{8}, $$ three eighths of the unit circle around it, which it touches at its four cusps (@fig-astroid). Its implicit form, $|x|^{2/3} + |y|^{2/3} \le 1$, shades the inside. :::figure[The astroid $(\cos^3 t, \sin^3 t)$ in the unit circle.]{#fig-astroid} ```plot |x|^(2/3) + |y|^(2/3) <= 1 {color=3 label="$A = 3\pi/8$"} x^2 + y^2 = 1 {color=1 .dashed label="$x^2 + y^2 = 1$"} (cos^3 t, sin^3 t) {color=4 label="$(\cos^3 t, \sin^3 t)$"} equal x in [-2, 2] y in [-1.25, 1.25] ``` ::: ## In short - Between graphs: top minus bottom in $x$, or right minus left in $y$, whichever needs fewer pieces. - In polar coordinates: $\frac12 \int r^2\,d\theta$, between the angles where the curves cross. - Inside a closed parametric curve: $\frac12 \oint (x\,dy - y\,dx)$.
